# Reggaeton

Genre numbers are orientation; the reference wins. Swing values: craft-groove-swing.

## When

Use it for reggaeton, Latin urban pop and Dominican dembow. Reggaeton is polished, with pads, piano and a clean tuned 808; dembow is faster, sparser and harder, with loud drums and little harmony.

## Tempo and drums

| Genre | Tempo, centre (range) | Kick | Snare / clap | Hats and other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reggaeton (dembow) | 95 (90–100) | 0, 4, 8, 12 | 3, 6, 11, 14 (or 3, 6, 9, 11, 14) | shaker in sixteenths, a fill every 4 bars |

- Reggaeton spans about 85–100. Dominican dembow runs about 115–130.
- The snare grid is the 3 + 3 + 2 cross-rhythm over a four-on-the-floor kick. Layer a snare and a rim on the same steps.
- Hats in eighths or sixteenths, kept simple; the shaker carries the sixteenths.
- Timbales or bongo fills at the end of 4- or 8-bar phrases: toms and congas from the Latin kit.
- Drop the dembow for a bar before the hook, then bring it back on the downbeat.

## Swing and groove

- Straight: `swing` 0. The groove is the 3 + 3 + 2 placement, not a shuffle.
- Keep the snare exactly on 3, 6, 11, 14. Moved to straight eighths, it loses the dembow.
- Accent the snare on 3 and 11 a little more than on 6 and 14; shaker accents on the beats.

## Harmony

- Minor keys. Typical loops: i–VI–III–VII, vi–IV–I–V, i–iv–VII–III.
- Four chords, one per bar, looping for the whole song; dembow often holds one chord or none.
- Piano or pluck plays the chords in a syncopated pattern that follows the dembow.

## Form

- Intro 4–8, Verse 16, Hook (coro) 8, Verse 16, Hook 8, Bridge 8, Hook 8, Outro 4. Usually 2:30–3:30.
- A short intro: streaming listeners want the beat fast.
- Changes every 4 bars are expected: a fill, a dropout, a new percussion layer.

## FX and mix signature

- Polished and clean in reggaeton: a short Plate on the snare, a Hall on pads, delay 1/8D on the lead.
- The 808 follows the kick; a light `duck` from the kick (2–4 dB) keeps them apart.
- Dembow: louder drums, a dirtier bass (808 `drive` up, Distorted preset), very little reverb.

## Reference core

1. The dembow: kick on every beat, snare on 3, 6, 11, 14.
2. A tempo near 90–100 (or 115–130 for dembow).
3. A tuned 808 or bass that follows the kick.
4. A short melodic loop or topline hook over a minor four-chord loop.

## Beginner mistakes

- Snare quantized to straight eighths: the 3 + 3 + 2 disappears.
- A long 808 that covers the kick.
- Busy hats that blur the dembow.
- A tempo too fast for reggaeton.

## Engine limits for this genre

- No vocals or autotune: a lead, vocal chops from the user's imported audio, or "hey" shouts as short synth stabs carry the topline (role-vocal-substitute).
- No timbales voice: toms and high congas stand in.
