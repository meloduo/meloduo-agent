# Pop-punk and rock

Genre numbers are orientation; the reference wins. Swing values: craft-groove-swing.

## When

Use it for pop-punk, punk rock, alt rock and emo-pop: fast, driving straight eighths, distorted power chords and a sing-along chorus. Slower alt rock uses the second tempo range. Classic and hard rock (mid tempo, riffs with space): genre-rock.

## Tempo and drums

| Genre | Tempo, centre (range) | Kick | Snare / clap | Hats and other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pop-punk, rock | 160 (140–190) or 100–130 | 0, 8 and syncopations | 4, 12 | eighths on hat or ride |

- Pop-punk usually sits at 160–190; many classics are near 174.
- Steps are 16th notes, 16 per bar: the beats are 0, 4, 8, 12 and the eighth-note offbeats ("and") are 2, 6, 10, 14.
- Default, the fast backbeat: kick on 0 and 8 (plus a syncopation such as 6 or 10), snare on 4 and 12, eighths on the hat. Crash or open hat in eighths in the chorus.
- Variant, the punk or polka beat (a hard, driving verse or chorus): kick on every beat 0, 4, 8, 12 and snare on the offbeats 2, 6, 10, 14, so the two alternate in eighths. Use it for a few bars of drive, not for a whole song.
- Half time for a pre-chorus or bridge: kick 0, snare 8.
- Fills on toms at the end of each 4- or 8-bar phrase; a crash on the first beat of every new section.

## Swing and groove

- Straight: `swing` 0 (craft-groove-swing). Tight to the grid is part of the style.
- Accents carry the energy: snare 115–127, hats alternate strong and weak, ghost notes rare.
- Velocity changes the tone on the Acoustic kit, so a hard chorus snare also sounds brighter.

## Harmony

- Major keys with I–V–vi–IV and its rotations; minor for darker alt rock (i–VI–III–VII).
- Power chords (root and fifth, often with the octave), down-picked in eighths.
- One chord per bar, or two per bar in a fast chorus. The bass plays the roots in eighths and doubles the guitar.

## Form

- Song form from stage-form: Intro 4, Verse 8–16, Pre 4–8, Chorus 8–16, Verse, Pre, Chorus, Bridge 8, a double Chorus at the end. Usually 2:30–3:30.
- The chorus opens up: crash and open hats, wider guitars, open chords instead of palm mutes.

## FX signature

| Genre | Signature | How |
|---|---|---|
| Rock | dense drums | comp Classic on drums, Room 0.6–1 s, sat on the drum bus, a Crush bus |

- Double-tracked rhythm guitars panned hard left and right (±0.8–1); bass and kick in the centre.
- Little reverb on the guitars; a short Room on the drums.
- Low cut on the guitars at about 80–100 Hz so the bass owns the low end.

## Reference core

1. Fast straight eighths with a punk beat or a driving backbeat.
2. Two distorted rhythm guitars, wide, playing the same power chords.
3. A chorus hook that lifts: crash, open chords, a lead line on the melody.
4. Picked bass locked to the kick and the guitar roots.

## Beginner mistakes

- One guitar track: no width. Always double.
- Too much amp `gain`: the chords turn to mush. Pop-punk needs less gain than metal.
- The bass fights the kick in the low end.
- Drums off the grid or with flat velocities.

## Engine limits for this genre

- No vocals: the hook goes to a lead guitar or a synth lead playing the vocal melody (role-vocal-substitute).
- No noise gate on the guitar: write clean note ends and rests instead.

The rock tempo and drum row is an estimate, not from sources.
