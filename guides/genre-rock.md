# Classic and hard rock

Genre numbers are orientation; the reference wins. Look a named band or track up first (craft-reference-analysis).

## When

Use it for classic rock, hard rock, blues rock, boogie rock and stadium rock of the 1970s–1990s. Faster punk, pop-punk and emo go to genre-pop-punk; low-tuned high-gain music to genre-metal.

## Tempo and drums

| Genre | Tempo, centre (range) | Kick | Snare / clap | Hats and other |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard rock | 120 (95–140) | 0, 8, often a push on 6 or 14 | 4, 12, hard and steady | 8ths on a half-open hat in the verse, ride or crash in 8ths or quarters in the chorus |

- Simple, heavy and on the grid: few ghost notes, few fills. A fill only at the end of an 8-bar phrase, often just a snare and floor-tom bar.
- A crash on beat one of every section; the chorus often moves the hat to the ride or crash.
- Stops: the band hits a chord together and leaves silence (whole bars of it before the last chorus).

## Swing and groove

- Mostly straight (`swing` 0); boogie and shuffle tracks swing the 8ths (craft-groove-swing).
- The groove is the riff against the backbeat. Leave rests in the riff: the space between the chords drives as much as the chords.

## Harmony

- Major and mixolydian (I–bVII–IV, I–IV–V, I–bIII–IV), blues (I–IV–V in 12 bars); minor pentatonic riffs over a major key are the genre's colour.
- Power chords and open chords (A, D, E, G with open strings), often hit and held, not strummed in constant 8ths.
- One or two chords per bar; the chorus often uses the same three chords as the verse, with more weight.

## Melody defaults

| Genre | Scale | Notes per bar | Range | Register | Phrase form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main riff | minor pentatonic + b3/3 blue note, mixolydian | 4–8, 8ths and 16ths with rests | 5–12 | 40–64 | a 1–2 bar riff, repeated, the last bar answers |
| Lead and solo | minor pentatonic, blues scale | 6–12, 16ths and triplets in bursts | 10–17 | 60–88 | short licks in the gaps; a solo of 8–16 bars that builds |

## Form

- Intro with the riff alone (often one guitar first, the band joins), Verse 8–16, Pre-chorus 4 (optional), Chorus 8, Verse, Chorus, Solo 8–16, Break or Bridge 4–8 with a stop, Chorus ×2, a big ending (a held chord with a drum roll). Usually 3:00–4:30.
- The riff is the hook: it opens the track, comes back between the sections and carries the chorus when there are no vocals.

## Palette

| Genre | Defining timbre | Roles usually present | Roles usually absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard rock | two crunch guitars with mids, a big dry kit, simple bass | rhythm guitar ×2 (one cleaner, one dirtier), lead guitar, picked bass in 8ths on the roots, live kit | synths, pads, arps, 808 |

## FX signature

| Genre | Signature | How |
|---|---|---|
| Hard rock | the band in a room | a Room reverb 0.8–1.5 s on the drum bus, little reverb on the guitars, a slapback echo (80–120 ms) on the lead |

- Gentle drum bus compression (2–4 dB); a heavy comp preset flattens the kick and the room. Check the gain reduction of every comp preset after the first render.
- Rhythm guitars hard left and right (±0.8–1), bass and kick in the centre, the lead slightly off centre.

## Mix

- The guitars are as loud as the drums: each rhythm guitar about −1…+1 LU against the kick, the snare the loudest drum. Guitars 3 LU under the kick sound like a demo of a pop track.
- Guitar tops: a High shelf on the guitar bus rather than more amp `gain`; more gain blurs the chords.

## Reference core

1. A memorable riff with rests, opening the track and coming back.
2. Two crunch guitars, wide, as loud as the drums.
3. A steady backbeat at mid tempo with few fills, a crash on each section.
4. A pentatonic solo before the last choruses.
5. Stops and a big ending.

## Beginner mistakes

- Taking pop-punk numbers: too fast, constant 8th strumming, no space.
- Too much gain: metal mush instead of crunch.
- Quiet guitars under loud drums.
- A busy drummer: rock drums are simple and heavy.

## Engine limits for this genre

- No vocals: the riff and the lead guitar carry the hook (role-vocal-substitute). Leave room for a vocal if the user may add one.
- No bends or vibrato on the guitar: a quick grace note a step below (a 1/32 note) stands in for a bend.
- No room microphones: a Room reverb bus on the drums stands in.

The tempo, harmony and mix numbers are estimates from typical records of the style, not measured from sources.
